## Part 1: What the prototype addresses, against the real user research

**Addressed well:**

- **The all-or-nothing fear.** Tom: "I got to a 12-day streak and I was proud
  of it... came back to a big fat zero." Amara: "I don't want to lose
  everything I've built after four days." The prototype directly answers this
  by showing the best-streak stat (12) instead of a reset to zero.
- **No way back in.** Tom: "There was no way to recover it, nothing. So I gave
  up." The prototype answers this with the one-tap freeze and the lesson as an
  actual re-entry path, not just an apology.
- **Acknowledgment builds attachment.** Priya: "hitting a 30-day streak and
  the app actually celebrating it, that little moment of 'look what you
  built.'" The done screen's "still yours to beat" line is a direct callback
  to that same acknowledgment pattern.

**Not yet addressed:**

- **Anticipatory anxiety, before any lapse happens.** Amara: "I'm already
  stressed about the streak, I keep thinking, what happens if I miss a day?...
  the pressure is starting to feel like a chore instead of a game." The
  Comeback screen only helps *after* a lapse. It does nothing for a user
  still in their first week who hasn't broken anything yet, which, per
  `docs/decision-brief.md`, is exactly the population Day-7 retention measures.
- **Trust in the freeze claim.** (Mock usability, Tom): "'no cost, no catch' is
  exactly what I'd expect an app to say right before it asks me to pay." Copy
  alone didn't resolve this until he actually tapped the button.
- **Track personalization.** (Mock usability, Priya): "I'm on the language
  track... doesn't quite know who I am." The lesson is fixed to miniature
  painting.

## Part 3: Structured design review doc (for sharing with Lena)

**What's working:** the acknowledgment-over-punishment framing lands against
all three interview subjects, not just the persona the feature was designed
around.

**What's not yet addressed:** trust in the freeze claim (needs a design fix,
not copy), track personalization (a scope decision), and anticipatory
anxiety before any lapse (a real gap, currently out of scope entirely).

**Single highest-impact change for week-1 retention:** not a tweak to this
screen, it's scoping a *proactive* reassurance moment shown before day 7,
reaching users who haven't lapsed yet. The current Comeback screen only
reaches users who already broke a streak, but Amara's profile, anxious from
day 4, never having broken anything, describes exactly the population Day-7
retention actually measures. A reactive-only fix structurally can't reach her.

**Product decision vs. Lena's decision:** whether to expand scope to a
proactive moment is a product/roadmap call (PM and Marcus), not a design
call. Lena's domain is the tone and interaction craft within whatever screens
get built, e.g., how the freeze resolves, how the lesson is framed, not
whether a new screen gets added to the roadmap.
